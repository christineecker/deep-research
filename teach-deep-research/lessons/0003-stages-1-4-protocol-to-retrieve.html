Lesson 3 of the deep-research course

Stages 1–4: the part that runs without asking you anything — until it does

After Stage 0, four stages run largely on autopilot: protocol, search, screen, retrieve. This lesson is about knowing what's happening while you wait, and recognizing the one moment this stretch does stop and ask — so you're not surprised by it.

Stage 1 — Protocol

The framework restatement you checked in Stage 0 becomes numbered inclusion/exclusion criteria here — built from your Stage 0 answers, never invented fresh at this point. Screening in Stage 3 will cite these criteria by number, so this is the last easy place to catch a wrong boundary. If your profile's gate includes protocol+strategy (standard and up), the protocol and the search strategy are shown to you and the run waits — your one editorial checkpoint before search starts spending queries.

Stage 2 — Search

Before writing a single new PubMed query, the run seeds from the shared pool — prior runs' papers scored against this run's question and filters. That's candidates only; Stage 3 still screens them fresh against the current protocol. Then it designs 4–8 genuinely orthogonal queries (not near-duplicates — see references/search-strategy.md) and logs every query string, its NCBI-translated form, and the hit count. At wide/max scope it adds Europe PMC and web sources on top of PubMed.

Stage 3 — Screen

Dedupe first (PMID → DOI → normalized title, in that priority order), then title/abstract triage against the numbered criteria from Stage 1. Every record gets include/exclude/unclear plus, on exclude, the first criterion it failed — so a screening decision is always traceable back to a specific rule, not a vibe. Retracted papers and Expressions of Concern are flagged here, not silently dropped.

At systematic/max, two independent screeners run plus an adjudicator on disagreement, and the agreement rate gets logged into the PRISMA flow. The included set is capped at your profile's max_articles, chosen by protocol relevance — the report states how the cap was applied, so "why isn't paper X in here" is always answerable.

Stage 4 — Retrieve, and the one interrupt

Every profile, fast included, walks the full open-access ladder for full text — there's no "narrow scope" shortcut that skips retrieval. What it can't get, it quarantines rather than guessing or faking. Once every selected record has been attempted, this is the moment the run stops and talks to you: a consolidated table in missing.md, covering both fully-quarantined records and abstract-only fallbacks (a stored abstract is not treated as good enough to skip this ask).

ProfileWhat happens at the halt
systematic / maxHard gate — extraction does not start until you resolve or explicitly wave it through
fast / standardMay proceed provisional on your say-so, gaps noted in the report

Your options at that prompt: drop a PDF into the run's inbox/ and let library.py ingest-inbox plus a rerun of acquire clear the block, or tell it to proceed provisionally (profile permitting) and accept the gap. What it will never do on your behalf: use saved credentials, an institutional proxy, a captcha solve, or a pirate mirror to get around a paywall — that's a hard invariant, not a setting.

The tangible win for this lesson: when a run pauses mid-flight showing you a missing.md table, you immediately know why it stopped, whether your profile lets you wave it through, and the two legitimate ways to clear it — instead of treating the pause as an error.

Check yourself
