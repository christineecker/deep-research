Lesson 4 of the deep-research course

Stages 5–6: one subagent per paper, and the pool that saves you the work

This is the expensive part of the pipeline — per-paper extraction and appraisal, done by dedicated subagents. It's also where the shared pool starts paying for itself, and where status.py --table becomes your checkpoint between the two stages.

Stage 5 — Extract

Before spending a subagent on a paper, the run checks the shared pool first (pool.py reuse). A hit means another run already extracted this exact paper — that extraction gets copied straight into this run, spans and all, and the subagent is skipped entirely. Only a miss dispatches fresh work: one opus subagent per paper, pulling design, sample size, population, intervention/comparator, outcomes (with effect, CI, and direction), funding/COI, limitations, and quoted spans with anchors.

One tag matters more than it looks: evidence_basis is set to abstract_only whenever full text wasn't obtained in Stage 4. That's not a footnote — Stage 6 refuses to appraise an abstract-only record as if it had full text, so a gap you waved through at the Stage 4 quarantine gate (Lesson 3) resurfaces here as a labeled limitation, not a silently degraded appraisal.

Recall from Lesson 2: if config.json has pipeline.stop_after_stage: 5, the run halts right here — no appraisal, no report. That's the "build the pool, decide later" move. Otherwise, once every paper is extracted, the pool gets synced (so future runs can reuse this run's fresh work) and the run posts a status.py --table summary.

Reading status.py --table

This is your coverage checkpoint before appraisal starts. It shows, per paper: what was downloaded and in what format (PDF / HTML / abstract-only text), and whether extraction actually ran — including which rows were reused from the pool versus freshly extracted this run. A run with several "reused" rows on a well-trodden topic is doing exactly what the pool is for; seeing everything come back fresh on a first pass through a topic is also expected and fine.

Stage 6 — Appraise

Same reuse gate, same idempotent check — safe to run even on a paper Stage 5 already reused, since it separately checks for a resolving appraisal. In standalone repo mode, appraisal is scoped to your specific project/manuscript (--project <slug>): a paper appraised for one question is not automatically treated as appraised for a different one, because "risk of bias" is evaluated against a specific outcome and comparator, not the paper in the abstract.

A miss dispatches, again, one opus subagent per paper. It first picks the appraisal tool that matches the study design — RoB2, ROBINS-I, Newcastle-Ottawa, AMSTAR-2, QUADAS-2, PROBAST, CASP, JBI prevalence/cross-sectional, or none for designs with no in-scope instrument (case reports, guidelines, narrative reviews) — then works that tool's domains, and finally rates per-outcome certainty with GRADE. (Full design→tool mapping: Pipeline Cheat Sheet and README's "Selection & appraisal criteria".)

The tangible win for this lesson: you can read a status.py --table output between Stages 5 and 6 and correctly say which papers are reused vs. fresh, which came back abstract-only (and therefore will get a lighter appraisal), and whether the run is actually ready to move into appraisal.
